# Hadrian Liadon
{: .no_toc }
*Elf fighter, gambler, and charming liar*

You've talked your way into card games, caravans and jobs you had no business holding. Waterdeep is the biggest table on the Sword Coast, and you know how to read the players.

## What every resident knows

### The city
- Waterdeep is the richest, largest city on the Sword Coast. It's divided into **wards**:
  - **Castle Ward:** government.
  - **Sea Ward:** wealth and temples.
  - **North Ward:** respectable homes.
  - **Trades Ward:** shops and guilds.
  - **Southern Ward:** caravans and workers.
  - **Dock Ward:** the harbor and rough streets.
  - **Field Ward:** poor and crowded.

  The **City of the Dead** is the walled cemetery. It's locked at night.
- The city is run by the secret **Masked Lords**. The one public lord is the **Open Lord, Laeral Silverhand**, who replaced **Dagult Neverember** a few years ago. For the past couple of months she has rarely been seen in public, and nobody will say why.
- Everyone's heard that Neverember **stole from the treasury** before he left. Nobody agrees on how much.
- The **City Watch** (green and gold) keeps the peace. The **City Guard** is the army. Both are mostly honest.

### Living here
- Coins: **nibs** (copper), **shards** (silver), **dragons** (gold), **harbor moons** (worth 50 dragons).
- Carrying a weapon is legal. Using one, or hurting people with spells, is not. Fines are the usual punishment.
- Nearly every trade has a guild. Guilds matter.
- **The Yawning Portal** is the famous adventurers' tavern with a well down into **Undermountain**, the Mad Mage's dungeon.
- **Blackstaff Tower** is home to the city's archmage, **the Blackstaff**, currently Vajra Safahr.
- Temples sell healing. With adventurers so scarce, they're readier than usual to help those who remain, especially in exchange for a favor.

### Dangers
- The **Xanathar Guild** is a criminal gang. People say its boss is a beholder.
- The **Zhentarim** are mercenaries with a bad history. They're currently trying to look respectable.
- The **Dock Ward** after dark is dangerous. The **City of the Dead** after dark is worse.

### The Death Curse
About three months ago, **the dead stopped coming back.** Resurrection magic fails everywhere. Worse, anyone who was ever raised from the dead is **slowly wasting away**, and nothing stops it.

- The city has poured money into finding a cure, so the treasury is strained.
- Many of Waterdeep's adventurers have left, or died chasing answers. The ones who remain are fewer and more careful.


### This winter
It's cold, the harbor is half frozen, and work is scarce. **Midwinter** festivities are coming, but they'll be subdued this year.

## You know how to work a room
*Because: Charlatan background · Persuasion, Deception and Insight*

Flattery opens more doors than threats. New money and old money alike are easy marks for someone who tells them what they want to hear.

- Waterdeep has around seventy-five noble families. Most made their fortunes in trade and keep villas in the Sea Ward and North Ward.
- Nobles hold no formal power over commoners. Their power is money, land, connections and the expectation that they'll get what they want.
- Noble youths ("the young bloods") are famous for parties, duels, and getting the Watch to look the other way.

- Etiquette: address a noble as "Lord" or "Lady" plus the house name until invited otherwise. Don't touch them uninvited. Never call a debt in public.
- Most houses have private guards and a steward who handles "small matters." Approach the steward first if you want something.
- **House Neverember** has never accepted Dagult's removal. Among themselves, and sometimes not only among themselves, the family talks about seeing him restored as Open Lord.

## You know where the games are
*Because: gambler by temperament · Sleight of Hand · Perception*

- **The Yawning Portal** in the Castle Ward is a famous tavern built around a huge well that leads into Undermountain. It's run by **Durnan**, a retired adventurer who doesn't tolerate trouble. Adventurers drink there before going down and celebrate there if they come back. Since the Death Curse, it's noticeably quieter: fewer adventurers, and more empty chairs raised in toasts.
- **Castle Waterdeep** dominates the slope of Mount Waterdeep: home of the City Guard and seat of government.
- **The Field of Triumph** in the Sea Ward hosts games, contests, executions and festivals.
- **The City of the Dead** is a walled cemetery. It's lovely by day, and nobody sane goes in after the gates close at sunset.
- **Mount Waterdeep** rises above the city, with the Castle and the harbor's watchtowers on its face.

- **Trollskull Alley**, in the North Ward, is a quiet lane of small shops and houses. An old tavern there has stood empty for years, and locals say it's haunted.
- **The Dock Ward** has many dockside taverns where you can hear news from every port in the world, and some where you can buy a stranger's silence.
- **The Market** opens at dawn, and its best stalls are sold out by noon. The crush peaks in mid-morning, when buyers race for what's left, and that's when pickpockets work it.
- **The Southern Ward** caravan yards are full of outlanders, and rooms there are cheap.
- **Gambling** is legal and everywhere: dice in Dock Ward taverns, wagers on the games at the Field of Triumph, and private card salons in the Sea Ward where the stakes are high and the guests are noble. Cheating is not legal, and the people you cheat rarely bother with magistrates.
- **Performers** make a living in the taverns and festhalls. The best stages pay in coin. The rest pay in meals and tips, and sometimes in the notice of a noble patron.

- Waterdeep has no single thieves' guild. Crime is split among rival gangs and organizations who don't trust each other.
- The **Xanathar Guild** is the best-known criminal gang. People say its boss is a beholder.
- The **Zhentarim** are mercenaries with a bad history, currently trying to look respectable.
- The Dock Ward after dark is dangerous.

- **The Xanathar Guild's** boss, "the Xanathar," is said to live somewhere beneath the city. Its thugs wear an eye-shaped symbol and run smuggling, protection and slaving out of the sewers and the Dock Ward. They're everywhere at street level: thugs, informants, sewer runners. Members say the boss is unpredictable and paranoid, and that failure is punished in creative ways.
- **The Zhentarim**, or Black Network, is an old organization of mercenaries, caravan guards and traders. In Waterdeep they pose as respectable sellers of protection and guard work. The Xanathar Guild sees them as a threat, and street talk says the two sides have been bloodying each other quietly.
- The Dock Ward after dark is where you go to buy what you shouldn't, and where you lose your purse if you're careless.
- **Fences** are the backbone of the trade. Most run a legitimate pawnshop or secondhand stall as a front and pay about a third of an item's value. They pay less for anything recognizable.
- The **Shard Shunners** are a gang of halfling thieves who work the sewers and cellars of the Dock Ward. They're small, organized, and good at disappearing. ⚠ verify whether you want them in your city.
- **Kenku** run messages and small jobs for whoever pays. They mimic voices, which makes them excellent spies and terrible witnesses.
- The City Watch tolerates a certain amount of vice in the Dock Ward. Violence and anything that touches nobles brings them down hard.

- The people you cheat go to people like the **Xanathar Guild**, whose thugs run protection and debt collection across the Dock Ward.
- The crowd left at the Yawning Portal is more careful than it used to be, and more willing to bet on long odds.
- Fences are useful when you've won something you'd rather turn into coin.

## You know caravan work and who competes for it
*Because: years as a (self-appointed) caravan guard · Intimidation*

- Nearly every trade in Waterdeep has a **guild**: carpenters, butchers, coopers, launderers, innkeepers, even wizards. Guilds set prices, train apprentices and protect members.
- The **harbor** is the city's lifeblood. Ships come from Amn, Calimshan, Baldur's Gate, Neverwinter and across the sea.
- **The Market** in the Castle Ward is the great open-air bazaar, where you can buy almost anything legal.

- Guilds are political. Guildmasters sit at feasts with nobles, and some are probably Masked Lords.
- The **Fellowship of Innkeepers** licenses taverns and inns. Open a tavern without their seal and you'll find the brewers won't sell to you.
- The **Cellarers' and Plumbers' Guild** maintains the sewers. Their members know the tunnels better than anyone above ground.
- Most guild dues run several dragons a year, plus a fee to register your shop. ⚠ verify exact dues if it matters for business plans.
- Caravans gather in the Southern Ward before heading inland. Caravan masters hire guards each season.

- **The Harpers** are a secretive network who oppose tyranny and protect the weak. They're more legend than fact to most folk, and they like it that way.
- **The Lords' Alliance** is a coalition of Sword Coast cities, including Waterdeep, Neverwinter and Baldur's Gate, which work together for mutual defense and trade.
- **The Order of the Gauntlet** is a holy order of knights and warriors who hunt evil. They're earnest, brave, and sometimes too eager.
- **The Emerald Enclave** are druids, rangers and others who protect the natural world. They're rarely seen in the city.
- **The Zhentarim** are mercenaries and traders with a dark history, now working hard to look respectable in Waterdeep.

- Faction membership is mostly informal: favors, contacts and shared goals. Members are expected to help each other.
- Harpers communicate through coded messages, and pass recognition through a small silver harp pin, which is rarely worn openly.
- The Lords' Alliance keeps an embassy and agents in Waterdeep. Waterdeep is its most powerful member, and the others resent that.
- The Zhentarim in Waterdeep offer guard work and caravan protection at reasonable prices. Their critics say it's a protection racket with good manners.

- A writ from a known guard company gets you hired. In Waterdeep, nobody checks too carefully whose name is on it.
- Unlike Cormyr, Waterdeep doesn't require adventurers to carry a **charter**. Your Cormyrean guild papers impress a few people and mean nothing legally.
- **The Zhentarim** undercut honest guard companies on caravan work. You've seen their "good manners" up close, and you've seen what's underneath them.
- **The Harpers** know you've done right by them. You're not one of them, but a Harper in Waterdeep might treat you as a friend.

## You had an education whether you wanted one or not
*Because: Arcana proficiency · a scholarly Evereskan upbringing*

- **Blackstaff Tower** in the Castle Ward is home of the **Blackstaff**, the city's archmage and protector. The current Blackstaff is **Vajra Safahr**, a young woman who took the title a few years ago.
- **Undermountain** is the vast dungeon beneath Mount Waterdeep, reached most famously through the well in the **Yawning Portal** tavern. Adventurers go down. Some come back.
- Anyone who wants to practise magic professionally in the city is expected to deal with the **Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors**.

- **The Watchful Order** licenses, polices and supports spellcasters. It also staffs the city's magical defenses and answers fire and magical emergencies. Members get access to shared libraries and spell supplies.
- **The Blackstaff** has traditionally been the Lords' magical right hand. Rumor says the Blackstaff commands a small team of elite adventurers on the city's behalf. ⚠ verify: Force Grey's public profile in your game.
- **Halaster Blackcloak**, the Mad Mage, still haunts Undermountain, by most accounts. The dungeon reshapes itself, and its magical gates lead strange places.
- Old wizards' towers are scattered across the city. Most are sealed or warded. Breaking in is foolish, and the wards are usually the least dangerous thing inside.

- **The dead stay dead.** Spells that bring the dead back to life no longer work. Priests of every faith have tried. The magic simply fails.
- **The wasting.** Anyone who was ever brought back from the dead is now slowly wasting away. Day by day they grow thinner and weaker, and no healing stops it. Some have already died, and they have not come back.
- **Nobody knows why.** Every temple, wizard and street-corner prophet has a theory. None of them has a cure.
- **The city has paid dearly.** Waterdeep has spent huge sums hunting for answers: hiring priests and sages, buying rare magic, funding expeditions. Many of the city's adventurers took that coin and left, or died trying. Many of the rest left on their own.
- **Adventurers are scarce**, so jobs that used to go to famous names now go to anyone willing. That means opportunity for new faces.
- **Healers and priests** are busy, expensive, and tired.
- **Fear makes people pious, reckless, or cruel.** Cults, charlatans selling "cures" and doom preachers are everywhere.

- The curse touches only those who were **brought back from death**. Ordinary healing and ordinary death work as they always did. The difference is that death is now final.
- The **wasting** works like a slow countdown. The stronger the victim, the longer it takes, but no one recovers. Greater restoration, remove curse and wish have all reportedly failed. ⚠ verify against your rulings.
- Temples are overwhelmed: the faithful demanding answers, families of the wasting begging for help, and donations drying up as the city's money goes elsewhere.
- Adventurers used to treat death as a setback. Now they don't, and fewer of them are willing to go into Undermountain or take dangerous jobs.

- Charming someone with magic inside the city walls is a crime, which is one more reason you rely on the ordinary kind of charm.
- With death permanent, adventurers have become cautious. That means fewer rivals for good jobs.

## You know your own people
*Because: Elf of Evereska · follower of Erevan Ilesere*

- Elves are a small population in the city, often artists, scholars or diplomats. Half-elves are common and fit easily into city life.

- Many of Waterdeep's elves have ties to **Evereska**, the hidden elven city in the mountains to the east. It's beautiful, ancient, heavily warded, and famously conservative. Evereskan families expect their children to spend their early centuries in study and duty.
- Elves in the city worship the **Seldarine**, the elven pantheon. Corellon Larethian leads it. Sehanine guides the dead. **Erevan Ilesere**, the trickster, is beloved by elves who can't sit still and distrusted by those who prefer they did.

- Word travels between elven households. A well-born runaway is exactly the sort of gossip that travels.
- The Seldarine have small shrines in the city rather than great temples. Followers of Erevan keep no temple at all. He is honored with pranks, gambles and stories, and his faithful tend to recognize each other.
- Elves live long enough that a family can lose track of a wayward child for decades. They also live long enough to come looking eventually.

- Waterdeep is tolerant. Almost any god may be worshipped, as long as its followers obey the law. Temples to evil gods are not welcome to operate openly.
- **The Plinth** is a simple open shrine to all gods, where anyone may pray.
- Two gods everyone in the city calls on:
  - **Tymora**, Lady Luck, for gamblers and adventurers.
  - **Waukeen**, goddess of trade, for merchants.
- With so few adventurers left in the city, temples are more willing than usual to help those who remain. A capable sword arm is worth a discount on healing when the clergy need something done.

- Waterdeep tolerates almost any faith that obeys the law. Nobody here will mind your god, though a few may watch their purses.

## Rumors you've heard
*Because: everyone hears something in a city this size*

- The **Temple of Beauty** is said to have a draught that slows the wasting. A vial costs a harbor moon, and the priests of Sune only sell it to families with a name.

## Languages
- **Elvish:** opens doors among Waterdeep's elves, and lets you talk freely in front of most humans.
- **Draconic:** the old language of magic and dragons. Wizards' texts and some old inscriptions use it.
- **Common.**

## Your story
- You're a long way from Evereska. Your family would want you back. Elves are patient, and Waterdeep is a city where Evereskan visitors are not unusual.
- You've annoyed gamblers and officials in other towns. Waterdeep is big enough to hide in, and busy enough that old faces turn up.
