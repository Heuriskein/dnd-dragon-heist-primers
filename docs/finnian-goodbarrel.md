# Finnian Goodbarrel
{: .no_toc }
*Halfling rogue, with a criminal past*

You've lived on the wrong side of Waterdeep's law, and it shows in how you see the city: who's watching, what's worth taking, and which doors stay unlocked.

- TOC
{:toc}

## What every resident knows

### The city
- Waterdeep is the richest, largest city on the Sword Coast. It's divided into **wards**:
  - **Castle Ward:** government.
  - **Sea Ward:** wealth and temples.
  - **North Ward:** respectable homes.
  - **Trades Ward:** shops and guilds.
  - **Southern Ward:** caravans and workers.
  - **Dock Ward:** the harbor and rough streets.
  - **Field Ward:** poor and crowded.

  The **City of the Dead** is the walled cemetery. It's locked at night.
- The city is run by the secret **Masked Lords**. The one public lord is the **Open Lord, Laeral Silverhand**, who replaced **Dagult Neverember** a few years ago. For the past couple of months she has rarely been seen in public, and nobody will say why.
- Everyone's heard that Neverember **stole from the treasury** before he left. Nobody agrees on how much.
- The **City Watch** (green and gold) keeps the peace. The **City Guard** is the army. Both are mostly honest.

### Living here
- Coins: **nibs** (copper), **shards** (silver), **dragons** (gold), **harbor moons** (worth 50 dragons).
- Carrying a weapon is legal. Using one, or hurting people with spells, is not. Fines are the usual punishment.
- Nearly every trade has a guild. Guilds matter.
- **The Yawning Portal** is the famous adventurers' tavern with a well down into **Undermountain**, the Mad Mage's dungeon.
- **Blackstaff Tower** is home to the city's archmage, **the Blackstaff**, currently Vajra Safahr.
- Temples sell healing. With adventurers so scarce, they're readier than usual to help those who remain, especially in exchange for a favor.

### Dangers
- The **Xanathar Guild** is a criminal gang. People say its boss is a beholder.
- The **Zhentarim** are mercenaries with a bad history. They're currently trying to look respectable.
- The **Dock Ward** after dark is dangerous. The **City of the Dead** after dark is worse.

### The Death Curse
About three months ago, **the dead stopped coming back.** Resurrection magic fails everywhere. Worse, anyone who was ever raised from the dead is **slowly wasting away**, and nothing stops it.

- The city has poured money into finding a cure, so the treasury is strained.
- Many of Waterdeep's adventurers have left, or died chasing answers. The ones who remain are fewer and more careful.


### This winter
It's cold, the harbor is half frozen, and work is scarce. **Midwinter** festivities are coming, but they'll be subdued this year.

## You know the underworld
*Because: Criminal background · Thieves' Cant · Expertise in Stealth and Sleight of Hand*

Freelancers like you get by on knowing whose turf you're standing on. Working Xanathar turf without permission is a quick way to get hurt.

- Waterdeep has no single thieves' guild. Crime is split among rival gangs and organizations who don't trust each other.
- The **Xanathar Guild** is the best-known criminal gang. People say its boss is a beholder.
- The **Zhentarim** are mercenaries with a bad history, currently trying to look respectable.
- The Dock Ward after dark is dangerous.

- **The Xanathar Guild's** boss, "the Xanathar," is said to live somewhere beneath the city. Its thugs wear an eye-shaped symbol and run smuggling, protection and slaving out of the sewers and the Dock Ward. They're everywhere at street level: thugs, informants, sewer runners. Members say the boss is unpredictable and paranoid, and that failure is punished in creative ways.
- **The Zhentarim**, or Black Network, is an old organization of mercenaries, caravan guards and traders. In Waterdeep they pose as respectable sellers of protection and guard work. The Xanathar Guild sees them as a threat, and street talk says the two sides have been bloodying each other quietly.
- The Dock Ward after dark is where you go to buy what you shouldn't, and where you lose your purse if you're careless.
- **Fences** are the backbone of the trade. Most run a legitimate pawnshop or secondhand stall as a front and pay about a third of an item's value. They pay less for anything recognizable.
- The **Shard Shunners** are a gang of halfling thieves who work the sewers and cellars of the Dock Ward. They're small, organized, and good at disappearing.
- **Kenku** run messages and small jobs for whoever pays. They mimic voices, which makes them excellent spies and terrible witnesses.
- The City Watch tolerates a certain amount of vice in the Dock Ward. Violence and anything that touches nobles brings them down hard.

- **Thieves' cant in Waterdeep:** chalk sigils on doorframes and gutters mark safe houses, Watch-heavy streets, generous marks and doors with dogs. A small circle with a line through it means "Xanathar territory, keep out."
- **Skullport**, the "Port of Shadow," is a lawless town deep beneath the city, reached through the sewers and tunnels. It's said to trade in anything, including people. Getting there is easy; getting back with your money is not.
- Underworld rumor talks constantly about **Neverember's missing gold**: a fortune the old Open Lord supposedly hid somewhere in the city. Every crook in Waterdeep claims to know someone who knows where it is. None of them do.
- The Death Curse has been **good for crime**. The Watch is stretched, the Lords are distracted, and there are fewer adventurers to hire against gangs. Fake "cures" and stolen temple relics are selling briskly.
- Smugglers move goods through the harbor at night in small boats, using sea caves and old cellars. The Watch doesn't patrol the water much.

## You know the halfling side of the city
*Because: Halfling ancestry · speaks Gnomish*

- Halflings live all over the city. Many have been here for generations. They run inns, bakeries, and shops, and keep many of the city's kitchens and cellars working.
- A small shrine to **Yondalla** serves the faithful, but most halflings worship at home and through hospitality.

- Halflings rarely cluster in a separate quarter, but they look after each other. A halfling can usually find a meal and a warm floor at another halfling's home, if they're polite and don't steal from their host.

- Halflings are underestimated, and some of them use that. The **Shard Shunners**, a gang of halfling thieves, work the sewers and cellars of the Dock Ward.

- Gnomes are drawn to the city's workshops, alchemists and wizards. Many work in the **Trades Ward** as tinkers, jewelers and lens-makers.

- Gnomes value clever work and good jokes in about equal measure. A non-gnome who speaks Gnomish will find them surprisingly warm.
- **The House of Inspired Hands**, Gond's temple, is popular with gnomish inventors.

- You know of the Shard Shunners, and they may well know of you.
- Gnomish is useful when you need a lock understood, or a trinket appraised quietly.

## You know some history
*Because: History proficiency*

- Waterdeep began as a trading post where northern tribes and southern ships met at the best harbor on the coast. It grew into a walled city ruled by warlords, and then by the secret Lords.
- **Ahghairon**, a legendary wizard, is credited with founding the Lords' system. His tower still stands in the Castle Ward, and nobody can open it.
- **Lord Dagult Neverember** was Open Lord for years and also ruled Neverwinter as its Lord Protector. He was removed a few years ago, and **Laeral Silverhand** took his place. He's still alive and still in Neverwinter.
- About three months ago the **Death Curse** struck. Since then the dead can't be raised, and the once-raised waste away. It's already the defining event of this year.
- Everybody has heard that Neverember **helped himself to the city treasury** before he was ousted. How much he took and where it went are the stuff of tavern arguments.

- **The Lords.** The Lords have been secret since Ahghairon's time, so that no one could bribe or threaten them. The Open Lord is the exception, the public face that negotiates with foreign powers.
- **The Spellplague and after.** About a century ago, magic itself went wrong across Faerûn. Waterdeep weathered it better than most. That period explains the odd ruins and abandoned towers you sometimes see.
- **Neverember's ouster.** He wasn't overthrown by the mob. The other Lords lost patience with an Open Lord who spent half his time in another city and spent the treasury freely. Laeral was chosen as a respected, steady replacement.
- **Laeral Silverhand** is one of the Seven Sisters, legendary chosen of the goddess Mystra, and is said to be centuries old. She once ruled Waterdeep in an earlier age, as the wife of the famous Lord Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun.
- **Halaster Blackcloak**, the "Mad Mage," built his lair beneath Mount Waterdeep long before the city grew. That is **Undermountain**.

- Waterdeep has around seventy-five noble families. Most made their fortunes in trade and keep villas in the Sea Ward and North Ward.
- Nobles hold no formal power over commoners. Their power is money, land, connections and the expectation that they'll get what they want.
- Noble youths ("the young bloods") are famous for parties, duels, and getting the Watch to look the other way.

Notable houses and what they're known for:

| House | Known for |
|---|---|
| Amcathra | Horse breeding, old military pride |
| Cassalanter | Banking and moneylending. Famously generous and pious, often hosting public feasts |
| Gralhund | A minor, ambitious house in the North Ward |
| Ilzimmer | Land, shipping, quiet influence |
| Margaster | Pottery and ceramics; known in the Trades Ward |
| Neverember | The ousted Open Lord's family |
| Rosznar | Wine. Once disgraced, quietly rebuilding |
| Thann | Vineyards and land outside the walls |
| Wands | A family of wizards; respected and a little feared |

- Etiquette: address a noble as "Lord" or "Lady" plus the house name until invited otherwise. Don't touch them uninvited. Never call a debt in public.
- Most houses have private guards and a steward who handles "small matters." Approach the steward first if you want something.
- **House Neverember** has never accepted Dagult's removal. Among themselves, and sometimes not only among themselves, the family talks about seeing him restored as Open Lord.

- With the treasury strained by the curse, **Neverember's stolen gold** isn't just a tavern story anymore. People with real power would very much like to find it.
- The city's old **thieves' guild** was destroyed by the Lords long ago. That's why the underworld is a free-for-all today.
- You know the major houses by name and reputation. Useful when choosing whose purse not to lift.

## You notice things
*Because: Investigation and Perception proficiency · Alert · a thief's habits (Criminal background)*

You also know the routes and shortcuts every working thief learns.

- **The Yawning Portal** in the Castle Ward is a famous tavern built around a huge well that leads into Undermountain. It's run by **Durnan**, a retired adventurer who doesn't tolerate trouble. Adventurers drink there before going down and celebrate there if they come back. Since the Death Curse, it's noticeably quieter: fewer adventurers, and those that remain are more subdued.
- **Castle Waterdeep** dominates the slope of Mount Waterdeep: home of the City Guard and seat of government.
- **The Field of Triumph** in the Sea Ward hosts games, contests, executions and festivals.
- **The City of the Dead** is a walled cemetery. It's lovely by day, and nobody sane goes in after the gates close at sunset.
- **Mount Waterdeep** rises above the city, with the Castle and the harbor's watchtowers on its face.

- **Trollskull Alley**, in the North Ward, is a quiet lane of small shops and houses. An old tavern there has stood empty for years, and locals say it's haunted.
- **The Dock Ward** has many dockside taverns where you can hear news from every port in the world, and some where you can buy a stranger's silence.
- **The Market** opens at dawn, and its best stalls are sold out by noon. The crush peaks in mid-morning, when buyers race for what's left, and that's when pickpockets work it.
- **The Southern Ward** caravan yards are full of outlanders, and rooms there are cheap.
- **Gambling** is legal and everywhere: dice in Dock Ward taverns, wagers on the games at the Field of Triumph, and private card salons in the Sea Ward where the stakes are high and the guests are noble. Cheating is not legal, and the people you cheat rarely bother with magistrates.
- **Performers** make a living in the taverns and festhalls. The best stages pay in coin. The rest pay in meals and tips, and sometimes in the notice of a noble patron.

- Watch patrols are thinnest where wards meet, and their timing is predictable within a ward.
- Dock Ward and Trades Ward rooftops are close enough to cross on foot if you're nimble.
- Many cellars connect to the sewers, and some connect to each other in ways no plan shows.

## Where the curse comes from
*Because: Investigation proficiency. You know people who know things.*

The temples and wizards are still arguing about causes. Follow the money and the expeditions, though, and the trail leads one way: **Chult**, the jungle peninsula far to the south, beyond Amn and Calimshan.

- The city's quiet expeditions have sailed for **Port Nyanzaru**, Chult's great harbor.
- Sages who've studied the curse keep circling back to that region.
- Few people know this, and fewer talk about it.
- The ships were quietly chartered with city coin. Some of the crews haven't come back.
- Smugglers and fences have noticed buyers paying silly money for anything out of Chult: charms, idols, maps, "cures."
- The few people in the know keep their mouths shut, because a secret that size is worth something.

You don't know *what* in Chult is causing it. Only that the answer lies there.

## Rumors you've heard
*Because: everyone hears something in a city this size · Investigation proficiency*

- The Xanathar Guild is jumpy. Its thugs are out in daylight in the Dock and Trades Wards, stopping anyone in black or wearing a winged-snake badge, and there's coin for anyone who can tell them where the Zhentarim are moving.
- A lamplighter on the Castle Ward rounds swears he's seen the Open Lord's plain black carriage cross to **Blackstaff Tower** after midnight, three nights running, and come back before dawn.

- **Common Sign Language:** handy for silent work.
- **Halfling** and **Gnomish:** see above.
