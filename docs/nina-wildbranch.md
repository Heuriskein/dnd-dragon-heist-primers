# Nina Wildbranch
{: .no_toc }
*Halfling druid, newly down from the wilds*

You've spent most of a long life deep in the forest, far from any town. Now you've come to Waterdeep for an adventure. It's loud, crowded and paved over, but people still get sick, the seasons still turn, and the gods still listen.

## What every resident knows

### The city
- Waterdeep is the richest, largest city on the Sword Coast. It's divided into **wards**:
  - **Castle Ward:** government.
  - **Sea Ward:** wealth and temples.
  - **North Ward:** respectable homes.
  - **Trades Ward:** shops and guilds.
  - **Southern Ward:** caravans and workers.
  - **Dock Ward:** the harbor and rough streets.
  - **Field Ward:** poor and crowded.

  The **City of the Dead** is the walled cemetery. It's locked at night.
- The city is run by the secret **Masked Lords**. The one public lord is the **Open Lord, Laeral Silverhand**, who replaced **Dagult Neverember** a few years ago. For the past couple of months she has rarely been seen in public, and nobody will say why.
- Everyone's heard that Neverember **stole from the treasury** before he left. Nobody agrees on how much.
- The **City Watch** (green and gold) keeps the peace. The **City Guard** is the army. Both are mostly honest.

### Living here
- Coins: **nibs** (copper), **shards** (silver), **dragons** (gold), **harbor moons** (worth 50 dragons).
- Carrying a weapon is legal. Using one, or hurting people with spells, is not. Fines are the usual punishment.
- Nearly every trade has a guild. Guilds matter.
- **The Yawning Portal** is the famous adventurers' tavern with a well down into **Undermountain**, the Mad Mage's dungeon.
- **Blackstaff Tower** is home to the city's archmage, **the Blackstaff**, currently Vajra Safahr.
- Temples sell healing. With adventurers so scarce, they're readier than usual to help those who remain, especially in exchange for a favor.

### Dangers
- The **Xanathar Guild** is a criminal gang. People say its boss is a beholder.
- The **Zhentarim** are mercenaries with a bad history. They're currently trying to look respectable.
- The **Dock Ward** after dark is dangerous. The **City of the Dead** after dark is worse.

### The Death Curse
About three months ago, **the dead stopped coming back.** Resurrection magic fails everywhere. Worse, anyone who was ever raised from the dead is **slowly wasting away**, and nothing stops it.

- The city has poured money into finding a cure, so the treasury is strained.
- Many of Waterdeep's adventurers have left, or died chasing answers. The ones who remain are fewer and more careful.


### This winter
It's cold, the harbor is half frozen, and work is scarce. **Midwinter** festivities are coming, but they'll be subdued this year.

## You know the gods and their houses
*Because: Religion proficiency · Hermit background · a follower of Chauntea*

- Waterdeep is tolerant. Almost any god may be worshipped, as long as its followers obey the law. Temples to evil gods are not welcome to operate openly.
- **The Plinth** is a simple open shrine to all gods, where anyone may pray.
- Two gods everyone in the city calls on:
  - **Tymora**, Lady Luck, for gamblers and adventurers.
  - **Waukeen**, goddess of trade, for merchants.
- With so few adventurers left in the city, temples are more willing than usual to help those who remain. A capable sword arm is worth a discount on healing when the clergy need something done.

Major temples, mostly in the Sea Ward and Castle Ward:

| Temple | God | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Spires of the Morning | Lathander | Dawn, renewal; a rich and popular temple |
| Tower of Luck | Tymora | Healing on credit for adventurers; luck charms |
| House of Wonder | Mystra and other gods of magic | Wizards and magic-users |
| House of the Moon | Selûne | Sailors, navigators, women, the lost |
| House of Inspired Hands | Gond | Inventors and artisans |
| Halls of Justice | Tyr | Law and judgment; works closely with magistrates |
| Shrine of the Suffering | Ilmater | Care for the poor and sick |
| Temple of Beauty | Sune | Art, love and lavish ceremony |

- Since the Death Curse, no temple can raise the dead, and every temple is besieged by the frightened and the wasting. Tempers among the clergy are short.
- Healing is for sale at most temples. The price drops sharply for the faithful, and for those who do the temple a favor.
- Chapels and shrines to small gods are scattered through the Dock Ward: sea gods like Umberlee get offerings from nervous sailors.

- Chauntea, the Great Mother of growing things, is a farmer's goddess, and Waterdeep is a city of stone. Her faithful here are mostly gardeners, market farmers and the folk who bring food in through the gates.
- A druid's devotion looks odd to city priests: no temple, no tithe, no vestments. Some will treat you as a curiosity, and some as a rival.

## You know the Death Curse as a healer
*Because: Medicine and Religion proficiency · Healer*

- **The dead stay dead.** Spells that bring the dead back to life no longer work. Priests of every faith have tried. The magic simply fails.
- **The wasting.** Anyone who was ever brought back from the dead is now slowly wasting away. Day by day they grow thinner and weaker, and no healing stops it. Some have already died, and they have not come back.
- **Nobody knows why.** Every temple, wizard and street-corner prophet has a theory. None of them has a cure.
- **The city has paid dearly.** Waterdeep has spent huge sums hunting for answers: hiring priests and sages, buying rare magic, funding expeditions. Many of the city's adventurers took that coin and left, or died trying. Many of the rest left on their own.
- **Adventurers are scarce**, so jobs that used to go to famous names now go to anyone willing. That means opportunity for new faces.
- **Healers and priests** are busy, expensive, and tired.
- **Fear makes people pious, reckless, or cruel.** Cults, charlatans selling "cures" and doom preachers are everywhere.

- The curse touches only those who were **brought back from death**. Ordinary healing and ordinary death work as they always did. The difference is that death is now final.
- The **wasting** works like a slow countdown. The stronger the victim, the longer it takes, but no one recovers. Greater restoration, remove curse and wish have all reportedly failed.
- Temples are overwhelmed: the faithful demanding answers, families of the wasting begging for help, and donations drying up as the city's money goes elsewhere.
- Adventurers used to treat death as a setback. Now they don't, and fewer of them are willing to go into Undermountain or take dangerous jobs.

- Families of the wasting are trying everything, and herbalists are asked for remedies daily. You know enough to see that nothing in your kit stops it.
- With the temples overwhelmed, a healer who doesn't charge temple prices will never be short of patients.

## You know the season
*Because: Nature proficiency · druid*

- Winters are hard: snow, ice and freezing winds off the Sea of Swords. Shipping slows, and parts of the harbor freeze.
- The city turns inward. Usually the taverns are packed all winter. This year the adventurers' haunts, like the Yawning Portal, have empty tables, with so many adventurers gone or dead. Nobles still hold their winter balls, and the poor of the Field Ward and Dock Ward struggle with cold and the price of firewood.
- **Midwinter** is the great festival at the turn of the year: feasting, gift-giving and noble masquerades.
- This year the Death Curse hangs over everything. Midwinter will be quieter, and many families are mourning someone who wasted away.

- Dock work dries up in winter. Idle sailors and laborers fill the dockside taverns, and petty crime rises.
- Firewood and coal merchants get rich; temples hand out food and blankets.
- The Watch works shorter patrols in hard weather, and certain kinds of business take advantage.

- You can read the weather off the sea and the sky better than most city folk, and you know which storms will close the harbor.

## You know your own people
*Because: Halfling ancestry*

- Halflings live all over the city. Many have been here for generations. They run inns, bakeries, and shops, and keep many of the city's kitchens and cellars working.
- A small shrine to **Yondalla** serves the faithful, but most halflings worship at home and through hospitality.

- Halflings rarely cluster in a separate quarter, but they look after each other. A halfling can usually find a meal and a warm floor at another halfling's home, if they're polite and don't steal from their host.

- Halflings are underestimated, and some of them use that. The **Shard Shunners**, a gang of halfling thieves, work the sewers and cellars of the Dock Ward.

- Halfling innkeepers and cooks hear everything said across their tables, and they're more likely to repeat it to another halfling.

## Rumors you've heard
*Because: everyone hears something in a city this size*

- A link-boy who lights a young noble home most nights says the man is **Renaer Neverember**, son of the ousted Open Lord. According to the boy, Renaer stayed behind when his father left for Neverwinter and swears he's done with him. But he has a whole new crowd of friends buying his drinks, not all of them nobles, and the talk is always about his father's gold. The boy is certain Renaer knows where it's hidden. Why else would he have stayed?

## Tools and languages
- **Herbalism Kit:** apothecaries and temple infirmaries are desperate for herbs this winter, and pay well for them.
- **Carpenter's Tools:** you built your own shelter once. In the city, carpentry is guild work, so you'd need the guild's leave to sell it.
- **Animal Handling:** stablehands, carters and caravan masters always need someone animals trust.
- **Druidic:** the secret tongue of druids. Few in the city could even recognise it.
- **Elvish, Halfling, Common.**
