# City Basics
{: .no_toc }

Waterdeep, the City of Splendors: the largest and richest city on the Sword Coast, built between a deep natural harbor and the bulk of Mount Waterdeep.

## Common knowledge
*Anyone who has lived here for a season knows this.*

- **The wards.** The city is divided into wards, each with its own character:
  - **Castle Ward:** government, Castle Waterdeep, the Palace, and the great open-air Market.
  - **Sea Ward:** wealthy villas, great temples, the Field of Triumph arena.
  - **North Ward:** comfortable, quiet, respectable. Minor nobles and prosperous merchants.
  - **Trades Ward:** shops, guildhalls, workshops, bustle.
  - **Southern Ward:** caravan yards, warehouses, working families, outlanders.
  - **Dock Ward:** the harbor, sailors, taverns, cheap rooms, and the city's roughest streets.
  - **Field Ward:** the newest district, crammed inside the old walls, poor and crowded.
  - **City of the Dead:** the walled cemetery, a park by day and locked at night.
  - **Undercliff:** farms and estates below the cliffs east of the walls.
- **Who rules.** The city is governed by the **Lords of Waterdeep**. Most of them are **Masked Lords**, whose identities are secret. They appear in public only in masks and robes. The one public face is the **Open Lord**, currently **Laeral Silverhand**, a powerful wizard who replaced Lord Dagult Neverember a few years ago. For the past couple of months she has rarely been seen in public.
- **Who keeps order.** The **City Watch** polices the streets. They are green-and-gold uniformed, carry horns to summon help, and are generally honest, if overworked. The **City Guard** is the army. They man the walls and the castle and rarely bother ordinary folk.
- **Law.** Waterdeep runs on the **Code Legal**. Disputes go before magistrates. Common punishments are fines, hard labor, flogging and exile. Murder and treason can end at the gallows. Slavery is outlawed. Carrying a weapon is legal; drawing it in anger is not.
- **Magic in the streets.** Casting harmful or charming spells on unwilling people within the city is a crime. The **Watchful Order of Magists and Protectors**, the wizards' guild, helps enforce it.
- **Money.** Foreign coin is accepted, but locals use Waterdhavian names:

  | Coin | Metal | Worth |
  |---|---|---|
  | Nib | copper | 1 cp |
  | Shard | silver | 1 sp |
  | Dragon | gold | 1 gp |
  | Harbor moon | platinum crescent with electrum inlay | 50 gp |
  | Taol | brass trade-bar with a hole | 2 gp (only good in Waterdeep) |

  ⚠ verify: the taol and harbor-moon values against the Dragon Heist book.
- **Calendar.** The year runs by the Calendar of Harptos: twelve months of thirty days, with festival days between them. Midwinter is the great feast at the turn of the year. ⚠ verify the current year (DR) for your table.

## Proficient
*You've paid attention, so you know how the city actually works.*

- The Watch is organized by ward. A **civilar** commands a ward's patrols, and the officer who calls you "citizen" is usually an armar or a plain watchman. Bribing the Watch happens but is risky. Many watchmen take pride in being the incorruptible ones.
- Magistrates can be reasoned with. Fines are far more common than prison, and a good word from a guild or noble patron can halve a sentence.
- Waterdeep has no city-wide thieves' guild. That was smashed years ago, so crime is split among gangs and outside organizations who don't like each other.
- Guild membership matters. Practicing a trade without your guild's blessing invites fines and visits from large men with hammers.

## Expert
*You know the city as a professional knows it.*

- The Masked Lords' identities are the city's favorite parlor game. Some are surely nobles and guildmasters. Some are rumored to be people nobody would suspect. Accusing anyone out loud is foolish.
- The sewers and cellars are maintained by the Cellarers' and Plumbers' Guild. Under them lie older tunnels nobody maps, and below those, Undermountain.
- Watch patrols are thinnest where the wards meet. Their routes change, but their timing tends to be predictable within a ward.
